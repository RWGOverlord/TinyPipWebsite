Tiny Pip Privacy Policy

Effective October 26, 2026

Tiny Pip is a small companion app for tasks and habits, published by Emberforge Works, LLC. This policy explains what happens to your information when you use the app, and, at the end, when you visit this website or join its waitlist. The short version for the app: what you put into Tiny Pip stays on your phone. The app has no accounts, doesn’t track you, and doesn’t show ads. The one exception is a feature you choose to use: when you ask Pip to help break a task into steps, that task’s words are sent to be turned into steps (see “AI help with steps”).

What Tiny Pip keeps, and where

Tiny Pip saves what you enter so it’s there next time you open the app:

  • your tasks, their steps and any notes you add;
  • your habits and what you’ve logged;
  • how you said you felt in mood check-ins, and the gentle patterns Pip notices from them (“insights”), which are worked out on your phone;
  • what you told Pip about yourself when you started (what you tend to put off, why, where you’d like support and your pace), which you can change in the app, and your weekly look backs (how the week felt, what went well, what to focus on);
  • reminders you ask for, and when;
  • focus timer sessions;
  • the name you give Pip, and your name if you tell Pip;
  • your game progress (levels, coins, items, adventures) and your settings.

All of this is stored only on your device, in the app’s private storage. It is never sent to us or to anyone else. We can’t see it, and we can’t recover it for you.

Backups. If your phone backs up apps (to your Google account on Android, or to iCloud on iPhone), Tiny Pip’s data may be included, so it can come back if you reinstall or change phones. That backup is handled by Google or Apple under your account and your phone’s backup settings; it is not sent to us. You can turn it off in your phone’s settings.

Saving a copy yourself. In Settings, Save my data puts everything above into one file and opens your phone’s share options, so you can save it wherever you choose (your files, a cloud drive, a message to yourself). Restore from a backup loads such a file, on this phone or a new one, after showing you what’s in it. The file goes only where you send it; it is never sent to us. It isn’t encrypted, so anyone who has the file can read it, mood check-ins included: keep it somewhere private.

AI help with steps (optional)

When you add a task, you can tap “Help me break this down” to have Pip suggest a few small steps. Only when you tap it:

  • What’s sent: just the words of that one task (and your time zone, to know when your week starts). Nothing else: not your other tasks, habits, notes, reminders or names.
  • Where it goes: to our server, run by Supabase, which passes the words to Anthropic, the company that makes the Claude AI model, to write the suggested steps. The steps come back to your phone, where you can change them before saving.
  • What we keep: not the task’s words or the steps. Our server keeps only a random ID for your phone (created the first time you use this feature, not linked to your name, email or anything else about you), when you asked, whether it worked, and its size and cost, so we can count the free weekly allowance and keep costs in check.
  • Anthropic processes the words to create the steps under its commercial terms, which don’t allow them to be used to train its models; it may keep them for a limited time for safety and abuse monitoring. See Anthropic’s Privacy Policy and Supabase’s Privacy Policy.

If you never tap “Help me break this down”, nothing in this section happens. Uninstalling Tiny Pip removes the random ID from your phone; to have what our server holds about it deleted, email us.

Beta stats and questions (test versions only, and only if you say yes)

While Tiny Pip is in testing, the app asks whether you’ll help improve it by sharing a little about how things are going. It’s off unless you say yes, saying no changes nothing else in the app, and you can turn it off any time in Settings → Beta. (If you said yes to an earlier version of this question, which covered game numbers only, the app asks you again before sending anything new.) If you say yes:

  • Game numbers: for each day, Pip’s level and your places’ levels, the XP you earned by kind (tasks, habits, quests, gifts, snacks, events) and how much the daily limit held back, the energy (AP) and coins you earned and spent and on what, your adventures (where, and for how much energy), what you found (counted by rarity), the snacks you used, and whether a double-XP weekend was on; and when a level goes up, which level, and how many days (and active days) since you started.
  • Counts of how you use the app: for each day, whether you opened it; how many tasks you added, started, finished and removed, and for each whether its steps came from the AI helper, a template, yourself or none; how many habit check-ins you made, on how many habits, and how many habit goals you met; how many focus sessions and their minutes; and how many reminders you made, opened from their notification, and finished (done, started, cleared). These are numbers only.
  • Your answers to Pip’s questions: now and then (at most two a week), Pip asks a short question on the home screen, such as whether a task’s steps helped you get started, whether checking in on habits has been helpful, or whether a reminder came at a good time, and a few questions after your first 3 and 14 days. Answering is optional; “Not now” and “No more questions” are always there, and putting a question away isn’t recorded as an answer. Your choice of answer is sent, with which question it was and, for the steps question, whether the steps came from the AI helper or a template.
  • Notes, only if you write one: each question has an optional “Tell us more” box (up to 500 characters), and Settings → Beta → Send beta feedback lets you send a note about a problem or idea. A note is sent exactly as you wrote it, so please leave out anything private. Nothing you’ve written anywhere else in the app is ever attached.
  • What’s never sent: the words of your tasks, steps, notes, habits or reminders, your moods, your answers to Pip’s getting-to-know-you questions, your names, or anything you type other than a note you choose to send.
  • Also with each report: days are counted from when you started using the app, not as calendar dates; plus the app version, the kind of phone (iPhone or Android) and whether you’re on Free or Plus.
  • How it’s linked: to a random beta ID made on your phone, shown in Settings. It isn’t linked to your name, email, the AI helper’s ID or anything else. (If you send beta feedback without having said yes, a beta ID is made then, just for that note.)
  • Where it goes: to our server (run by Supabase), where it can only be added to, not read back by the app. Only the Tiny Pip team can read it, and we use it only to improve Tiny Pip: never for advertising, never sold, and never used to train AI.
  • Deleting it: Settings → Beta → Delete my beta data deletes everything sent from your phone at once and turns sharing off; your tasks, habits and game progress stay. Turning sharing off stops anything more being sent right away. You can also email us your beta ID.
  • How long: we delete all beta data within 90 days after Tiny Pip’s public launch.
  • The released app doesn’t have any of this.

What Tiny Pip doesn’t do

  • No sign-up or account.
  • No analytics, tracking or advertising, and no data sold or shared. (The one exception is the opt-in beta stats and questions above, in test versions only.)
  • No location, contacts, photos, camera or microphone.
  • No access to anything on your phone outside Tiny Pip.

Notifications

If you allow notifications, Tiny Pip schedules them on your phone: reminders you ask for, Pip’s check-ins, the focus timer and, if you keep it on, one evening mood check-in reminder. They’re created and delivered on your device; nothing is sent to a server to make them work.

Support resources

If something you type suggests you might be going through a very hard time, or you open “Need support?” in the menu, Tiny Pip shows free, confidential helplines. That check happens on your phone, and nothing about it is sent anywhere. Tapping a helpline opens your phone’s own messages, phone or browser; Tiny Pip doesn’t see what happens there.

Feedback

The app has a “Send feedback” button. In test versions it opens Send beta feedback (see Beta stats and questions), with a button to email us instead. Email opens your own email app with a message addressed to us and fills in the app’s version and your phone’s model, which help us fix problems. Nothing is sent unless you send it. If you do, we receive your email address and whatever you write, and we use them only to reply and to improve Tiny Pip. We don’t add you to any mailing list. Ask us any time and we’ll delete your messages.

Google Play, the App Store and TestFlight

Tiny Pip is distributed through Google Play and Apple’s App Store, and test versions through Google Play testing and Apple’s TestFlight. Google and Apple may collect information about installs and app crashes under their own privacy policies and share crash reports with us in summary form; these don’t include what you’ve entered in the app. If you join a test, the store tells us the email address you were invited with, and TestFlight passes along any feedback or screenshots you choose to send. See Google’s Privacy Policy and Apple’s Privacy Policy.

Purchases

Tiny Pip doesn’t take payments at this time. If paid features are added, purchases will go through Google Play or the App Store, and we’ll update this policy first.

Children

Tiny Pip isn’t directed at children under 13, and we don’t knowingly collect information from them.

Deleting your information

Uninstalling Tiny Pip deletes everything it has stored on your phone, and so does Settings → Your data → Delete everything and start over (it asks twice first). To remove it from your phone’s backup too, use your phone’s backup settings. To delete feedback you’ve emailed us, what our server holds for the AI helper, or your beta data (Settings → Beta → Delete my beta data does it at once, or send us your beta ID from there), write to the address below; we delete it within 30 days. The steps are also on Delete your data.

Changes

If this policy changes, we’ll update it here and change the date at the top. If a change affects what happens to your information, we’ll say so in the app before it takes effect.

This website and the waitlist

This website, heytinypip.com, has no accounts and no ads.

Analytics. We use Google Analytics to understand how many people visit and how they find us: which pages are viewed, the link that brought you here (for example, TikTok), your general location (country or city), device and browser, and how you use the page, such as how far you scroll, which buttons you tap, and whether you joined the waitlist and picked iPhone or Android. It doesn’t tell us who you are, and we never send it your email address. Google Analytics sets cookies to tell visits apart; in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland, it runs without cookies. We don’t use it for advertising. Google handles this data under the Google Privacy Policy; see also how Google uses information from sites that use its services. You can opt out with Google’s opt-out browser add-on, or by blocking cookies or trackers in your browser.

The waitlist. If you join the waitlist, we collect your email address and whether you use an iPhone or Android. We use them only to tell you about Tiny Pip’s launch and early access, such as inviting iPhone and Android testers in separate groups. We never sell or share them. The list is kept by Mailchimp, our email service, which handles it for us under its own privacy policy; Mailchimp may record whether our emails are opened and which links are clicked. Every email has an unsubscribe link, so you can leave the list anytime. To have your details deleted completely, email us at the address below.

Hosting. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages. Like most web hosts, GitHub may log visitors’ IP addresses to keep the service secure and running, under the GitHub Privacy Statement. Apart from GitHub and Google Analytics, the site loads nothing from other companies until you join the waitlist, which sends your details to Mailchimp.
