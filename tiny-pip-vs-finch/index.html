Tiny Pip vs Finch: which companion app fits you?

Both apps give you a small creature that cheers you on and goes on adventures when you take care of things. They’re built for different problems, though. This page is about which kind of companion you actually need.

By the Tiny Pip team Updated November 19, 2026 6 min read

The short answer: choose Finch if you want a broad self-care routine, with daily goals, journaling prompts, breathing exercises, mood tracking and friends. Choose Tiny Pip if your main struggle is starting: you know what you need to do, but the bill, the email or the messy room keeps not happening. Tiny Pip shows one task at a time, turns it into a tiny first step, and rewards you most for beginning.

A note on fairness: we make Tiny Pip, so we’re not neutral. Everything we say about Finch comes from Finch’s own website and App Store listing, checked on November 19, 2026. Who Finch is a good fit for

Finch describes itself as “a self-care pet app that helps you take small steps for yourself, with a little bird cheering you on.” It covers a lot of ground: goals to check off, reflection prompts, guided breathing, soundscapes, a mood tracker, quizzes, seasonal events and friends who can send each other encouragement.

Finch may suit you better if:

  • You want one app for your whole self-care routine, including journaling and breathing exercises.
  • You like writing reflections and having prompts to guide you.
  • You’d enjoy doing it with friends and sending each other good vibes.
  • Your goals are mostly small daily self-care habits, like drinking water or stepping outside.
  • You want an app with a long track record and a large community.

Who Tiny Pip is a good fit for

Tiny Pip is narrower on purpose. It’s a gentle productivity and self-care companion built around one moment: when you know exactly what to do and still can’t start.

Tiny Pip may suit you better if:

  • Your problem is starting real tasks: bills, emails, calls, chores, work or school, not remembering them.
  • A long list makes you freeze, and you’d rather see one thing at a time.
  • You want help breaking a big task into a tiny first step, with steps you can edit. How breaking tasks down works.
  • You want starting to count. In Tiny Pip, tapping “I’ve started” earns more than finishing.
  • You want rewards for real life, not screen time. Pip explores while the app is closed, and opening the app earns nothing.
  • You want no streaks and no social pressure. Why Tiny Pip is built to be gentle.
  • You’d rather have no account. Tiny Pip keeps what you enter on your phone.

How the adventures differ

Both apps turn what you do into a little journey for your companion, but the loops are shaped differently.

In Finch, completing goals gives your bird energy, and once it has enough, it sets off on an adventure and comes back with a story to share.

In Tiny Pip, starting tasks, finishing steps and logging habits earn Adventure Points. You choose where Pip goes, starting in the Backyard, and for how long, from 5 minutes to an hour. Every few minutes of exploring is a chance to find something: items to collect, outfits to wear, coins, and now and then a pet. Each place has 25 levels and gets luckier the more Pip explores it. More about Pip’s adventures.

Can you use both?

Yes. They solve different problems, so some people may use Finch for reflection, breathing and mood tracking, and Tiny Pip for getting specific tasks started. The two apps don’t connect, so you’d keep them separately.

Still not sure?

Ask yourself one question: when a day goes badly, is it because you didn’t look after yourself, or because you couldn’t make yourself start the thing? If it’s the first, a broad self-care app like Finch is likely the better match. If it’s the second, try Tiny Pip. Both are free to try, so you can always test them side by side.

Questions

Is Tiny Pip better than Finch?

Neither is better for everyone. Finch is a broad self-care app with goals, journaling prompts, breathing exercises, mood tracking and friends. Tiny Pip focuses on one problem: starting tasks you keep putting off. Choose the one that matches what you struggle with most.

What is a good Finch alternative for procrastination?

If your main struggle is getting started on real tasks, Tiny Pip is built for that. It shows one task at a time, suggests a tiny first step, can help break a bigger task into steps, and rewards you more for starting than for finishing.

Do both apps have a pet that goes on adventures?

Yes. In Finch, completing goals gives your bird energy for an adventure, and it comes back with a story. In Tiny Pip, real progress earns Adventure Points; you choose a place and how long Pip explores, and Pip comes back with items, outfits, coins and sometimes a pet to collect.

Can I use Tiny Pip and Finch together?

Yes. Some people may like Finch for reflection, breathing and mood tracking, and Tiny Pip for getting specific tasks started. They don’t connect to each other, so you’d use them separately.

Are Tiny Pip and Finch free?

Both are free to download and use, with an optional paid plan. Finch’s core self-care features are free, and Finch Plus adds extras. Tiny Pip is free and in beta now; its optional Plus plan is coming at launch.

Is either app a mental health treatment?

Tiny Pip is a productivity and self-care app, not therapy or medical care. Neither app replaces support from a doctor or mental health professional.
