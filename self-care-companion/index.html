A self-care companion

Self-care doesn’t have to become another checklist

Drink more water. Meditate. Journal. Go outside. Self-care advice can pile up until it looks a lot like a to-do list, and then it’s one more thing you’re behind on. Tiny Pip keeps self-care small, optional and kind.

Looking for a gentle self-care app without streaks? Tiny Pip is a small companion for everyday tasks and self-care. Pip checks in on how you feel (you can always skip), suggests one small kind idea on a hard day, switches to comfort mode when you’re sick, and helps with habits without ever counting what you missed.

A check-in that takes a second

In the morning and the evening, Pip can ask how you feel. There are five faces: bad, meh, okay, good and great. Tap one, and that’s it. There’s nothing to write, and Skip for now is always right there.

If you tap bad or meh, Pip doesn’t try to fix your day. Pip offers one small, kind idea, something you could do in a minute or two, and asks, “Need to talk to someone?” in case you do.

After about five days of check-ins, Pip may notice a gentle pattern or two, like how your days tend to feel. Pip keeps it to a couple at most, and works them out on your phone. Your moods aren’t sent anywhere.

Reminders for check-ins only come if that check-in isn’t done yet, and you can turn them off in Settings. Skipping one is just skipping one.

  • When you’re under the weather

    Tap I’m feeling sick on the check-in, and rest becomes the day’s job. Pip offers one-tap comfort ideas like a nap, a warm shower, tea or a glass of water. Each one counts as progress and earns Adventure Points, and sick days count toward your week. After a few days, Pip asks if you’re feeling any better.

  • Small kind ideas

    If you add a task with words like “tired,” “overwhelmed” or “anxious,” Pip suggests three small, kind things you could do instead or first. You can take one, or carry on with your task.

  • A weekly look back, if you want one

    On Sunday afternoons, Pip invites you to look back on the week: how it felt, what went well, what you’d like to focus on. Choose “Not this week” and it waits until next Sunday. On Monday, Pip can suggest a small habit for the focus you picked.

  • Rest that counts

    Taking a break is always one of the choices. When you do, Pip says, “Rest is part of it. I’ll be here when you’re ready.” More about rest and gentle productivity.

Self-care habits, without the scorecard

Habits in Tiny Pip can be once a day, like taking a walk, or measured, like glasses of water or minutes of reading. Today’s habits sit on Home as little rings, and one tap logs a glass, starts a timer or marks it done.

Not sure where to start? Pip has dozens of small self-care ideas to pick from, in areas like calm, rest, self-kindness, moving, getting outside and connecting with people. You decide which days a habit is for, so a weekday habit doesn’t show up on your weekend.

Forgot to log yesterday? Fill it in. Didn’t do it at all? Nothing resets. Every bit of a habit you do log, even three glasses out of eight, is still progress Pip celebrates.

You can also add medicines and vitamins with your own reminder times. They’re reminders, not medical advice, and they don’t count toward the free habit limit.

Self-care includes the things you’ve been avoiding

Sometimes the kindest thing you can do for yourself is finally deal with the bill, the email or the appointment that’s been hanging over you. That’s the other half of Tiny Pip: it helps you pick one thing and take a tiny first step.

Starting is celebrated more than finishing, so you can stop whenever you need to, leave yourself a note, and pick it back up later. Each real step you take earns Pip time to go exploring. How Tiny Pip helps you start.

What Tiny Pip is, and isn’t

Tiny Pip is an everyday companion for tasks, habits and small acts of self-care. Pip is encouraging, not clinical, and Pip doesn’t pretend to be a counselor.

Tiny Pip is not therapy, medical care or mental-health treatment, and it isn’t diagnostic software. It doesn’t assess, diagnose or treat any condition.

If you type words in Tiny Pip that suggest you might be in crisis, Pip shows ways to reach support right away: in the US, call or text 988, or text HOME to 741741 for the Crisis Text Line; elsewhere, findahelpline.com lists local lines. If you’re struggling, please reach out to someone you trust or a local support line.

Questions

Is Tiny Pip a mental health app?

No. Tiny Pip is a productivity and self-care app for everyday tasks and habits. It isn’t therapy, medical care or mental-health treatment, and it doesn’t diagnose anything. If you’re struggling, please reach out to someone you trust or a local support line.

Do I have to do the mood check-ins?

No. Every check-in has “Skip for now,” and a skipped check-in is just skipped. You can also turn the check-in reminders off in Settings.

Where are my moods kept?

On your phone. Your check-ins and the patterns Pip notices from them are worked out and saved on your phone, and Tiny Pip has no account.

What if I’m sick?

Tap “I’m feeling sick” on the check-in. Pip switches to one-tap comfort ideas like a nap, a warm shower, tea or water. They count as progress, and sick days count toward your week.

Does self-care in Tiny Pip have streaks?

No. There are no streaks anywhere in Tiny Pip. Missing a check-in, a habit or a whole day doesn’t reset anything.

Does Tiny Pip use AI to talk about my feelings?

No. Check-ins, suggestions and patterns all work on your phone without AI. The only AI feature is the optional “Help me break this down” button for splitting a task into steps.
