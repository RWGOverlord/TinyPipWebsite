What Tiny Pip does

Everything Pip does, built around the hardest part: starting

Most to-do apps help you remember what to do. Tiny Pip is for when you already know, and still can’t begin. It helps you pick one thing, find a first step small enough to take, and feel good about taking it. Your real progress then sends Pip, your small companion, off on adventures.

In short: Tiny Pip is a gentle productivity and self-care companion. It turns the thing you keep putting off into one tiny first step, rewards you for starting, not just finishing, and never punishes you for missing a day.

When starting is hard

Make the first step small enough to take

Starting is where most of us get stuck, so that’s where Tiny Pip puts its help.

One thing at a time

A long list can make starting harder: every item asks for attention at once. So Tiny Pip’s home screen doesn’t show your list. Pip suggests one thing, like a task you started earlier, one that’s waiting, or a habit to log, with a big Let’s start button and an easy way to pick something else.

Your full list is still there when you want it, a tap away under See my tasks & habits. Tasks can be personal, for work or for school, and there’s no limit on how many you add.

Tiny first steps

When you add a task, Pip asks whether it’s quick, a few steps, or something you just need to put time into. For everyday things like paying a bill, canceling a subscription, replying to a message, laundry, dishes or tidying up, Pip offers a ready-made set of small steps. You say yes, change them, or write your own.

For a quick task, the step is just doing it. For anything bigger, the first step is deliberately small: open the account, find the email, clear one counter.

Help breaking it down

For a task without a ready-made plan, tap Help me break this down. An AI helper turns it into 3 to 7 small steps, or one tiny step for a quick task. If the task is unclear, it asks you one question first.

Nothing is added until you say so. You can edit any step, move it up or down, remove it or add your own before saving. Only that one task’s words are sent, and they aren’t kept. Free includes a few breakdowns a week; Plus will have no weekly limit. More about breaking tasks down.

Starting counts more than finishing

Tap I’ve started and Pip cheers. In Tiny Pip, starting a task earns more than finishing it, and your first start of the day earns enough Adventure Points for a whole trip on its own. Finishing still counts, and so do the steps in between.

Already did it? Already done counts too. And when you need to stop, Done for now is always one of the choices.

A short focus timer

Choose 10, 20 or 30 minutes, or no timer at all. Pip shows the step you’re on and stays quiet. You can pause or stop anytime, and you can put your phone down: Tiny Pip lets you know when time’s up.

When the timer ends, Pip asks if you want a break, and you can take one, keep going, mark your progress or switch to something else.

Pick up where you left off

Bigger tasks rarely happen in one go. When you take a break, Pip offers to save a note for next time. Later, Pip asks if you want to pick it back up, and shows your next step and your note, so you don’t have to work out where you were.

When productivity feels bad

No guilt, no pressure, no broken streaks

A lot of productivity tools run on pressure. Tiny Pip is built to do the opposite. Why we built it this way.

Rest is allowed

Stopping is always one of the choices, never a failure. When a focus timer ends, when you finish a task, or when you stop partway, Pip offers a break alongside “keep going.” Take it, and Pip says: “Rest is part of it. I’ll be here when you’re ready.”

Rewards also level off after a busy day, so there’s nothing to grind for, and Pip never nudges you to do more.

  • No streaks to lose

    There are no daily streaks. Missing a day doesn’t reset anything, and when you come back, Pip is just happy to see you.

  • A few good days, not every day

    Home shows your week, Monday to Sunday. Any real progress makes a day count. The goal is 3 days a week, and you can choose anywhere from 2 to 7. Each Monday starts fresh.

  • Gentle reminders

    Ask for a reminder whenever you like, once or repeating. You choose how often Pip checks in, from once a day to only when you ask. Quiet hours are on by default, and once you’ve made progress, Pip stops nudging for the day. Pip never brings up missed days or overdue tasks.

  • Feeling under the weather

    Tap I’m feeling sick and Pip switches to one-tap comfort ideas, like a nap, a warm shower, tea or water. They count as progress, and sick days count toward your week.

Self-care

Check in with how you feel

Morning and evening, Pip can ask how you feel: bad, meh, okay, good or great. It takes a second, and you can skip it. On a harder day, Pip suggests one small, kind idea. After a few days, Pip may notice a gentle pattern or two, worked out on your phone.

On Sundays, Pip invites you to look back on your week, if you want to. More about self-care in Tiny Pip.

Tiny Pip is a productivity and self-care app, not therapy or medical care. If you’re struggling, please reach out to someone you trust or a local support line.

Your companion

Meet Pip

Pip is a small green companion who cheers when you start, rests when you rest, and goes exploring on your behalf. You can rename Pip, dress Pip up, and unlock new looks as Pip levels up.

Your progress becomes Pip’s adventure

Starting tasks, finishing steps and logging habits earn Adventure Points. Spend them to send Pip exploring, for 5 minutes up to an hour. You can close the app: Pip keeps exploring and lets you know when Pip’s back.

Every few minutes of exploring is a chance to find something: items to collect, outfits to wear, coins, and sometimes a pet. Time spent in the app earns nothing. Only what you do in real life sends Pip anywhere. More about Pip’s adventures.

Places to explore

Pip starts in the Backyard. With Plus, the Forest, the Mountain and the Castle open too. When Tiny Pip launches, three more free places open: Candy Meadow, the Pirate Ship and the Crystal Cave.

Each place has 25 levels. The more Pip explores somewhere, the luckier the finds get there, and every fifth level brings a guaranteed outfit piece. On some weekends, rare finds are three times as likely everywhere.

Collections, outfits and pets

Each place has 20 items to find, from common to legendary, and four pets. Many items are things Pip can wear, like hats, wings, backpacks and capes. One pet at a time can tag along with Pip, like the Backyard’s bumblebee.

Coins from adventures buy more outfits in the shop. The shop only takes coins, never real money. There are also badges, small daily goals and weekly quests, and a daily gift that never resets if you miss a day.

Building routines

Habits that fit real life

Add a habit you do once a day, or one you measure, like glasses of water or minutes of reading. Today’s habits sit on Home as little rings you can tap. Pick which days they’re for: every day, weekdays, weekends or your own choice. Pip also has dozens of small self-care ideas to start from.

Forgot to log yesterday? You can still fill it in. Free includes up to 10 habits, and medicine and vitamin reminders don’t count toward that.

  • Less time in the app, not more

    Tiny Pip never rewards screen time. Opening the app earns nothing, Pip explores while the app is closed, and the point of every screen is to get you back to your real day.

  • No account, and it stays on your phone

    There’s nothing to sign up for. Your tasks, habits and check-ins are saved on your phone. The one exception is the optional AI helper, which sends just that one task’s words. Privacy policy.

AI help

What the AI does, and what it doesn’t

Tiny Pip uses AI for one thing: when you tap Help me break this down, it suggests small steps for that task. Everything else, including Pip’s ready-made steps, reminders, check-ins and the game, works on your phone without AI.

  • It only runs when you ask, one task at a time.
  • It suggests steps; you decide which ones to keep.
  • It isn’t a coach, a counselor or a therapist, and it doesn’t chat with you about your feelings.
