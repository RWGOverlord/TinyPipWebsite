ADHD-friendly

A gentler way to start when your brain says “not yet”

Many people with ADHD describe the same frustrating gap: knowing exactly what needs doing, wanting to do it, and still not being able to begin. Tiny Pip was designed around that gap.

The short answer: Tiny Pip is a task app designed around starting, the part many people with ADHD describe as hardest. It shows one task at a time, turns it into a tiny first step, offers short focus sessions, and has no streaks to break. It’s a productivity and self-care app, not ADHD treatment.

Tiny Pip is a productivity and self-care app, not ADHD treatment or medical care. It doesn’t diagnose, treat or replace anything a doctor, therapist or coach provides. It’s a small companion for getting started on everyday tasks.

What many people describe

Talk to people with ADHD about to-do apps and some of the same frustrations come up again and again:

  • Starting is the wall. The task is clear and important, and it still doesn’t happen. Often this is called task initiation, or “task paralysis.”
  • Long lists backfire. Writing everything down feels productive, then the list becomes one more thing to avoid looking at.
  • Interruptions erase progress. After a break, it’s hard to remember where you were, so restarting feels like starting from scratch.
  • Streaks turn into guilt. A broken streak can make it feel like the whole system has failed, and it’s easier to abandon the app than to come back.
  • Notifications pile up. Constant nudges get tuned out, or worse, start to feel like nagging.

None of these are unique to ADHD. Lots of people struggle with starting. But they shaped a lot of Tiny Pip’s design choices.

Designed around task initiation and overwhelm

Here’s how each of those struggles shows up in the app.

One thing at a time, not a giant list

Tiny Pip’s home screen shows one suggestion with a big Let’s start button. There are no overdue badges and no wall of tasks. Your full list exists, but you only see it when you go looking.

Tiny first steps, and help breaking things down

For common chores, Pip suggests small ready-made steps. For anything else, you can tap Help me break this down and an optional AI helper suggests a few small steps you can edit, reorder or remove. Free includes a few AI breakdowns a week. How it works.

Starting counts most

In Tiny Pip, starting a task earns more than finishing it. Tap I’ve started and Pip cheers right away. That quick, visible reward is there for the moment you need it most: the beginning.

Built for interruptions

Stop whenever you need to. Pip offers to save a quick note, and next time shows your next step and that note, so coming back doesn’t mean starting over. A short focus timer of 10, 20 or 30 minutes is there when you want structure, and Pip asks about a break when it ends.

No guilt, and fewer nudges

  • No streaks

    There’s nothing to break. Home shows how many days you made progress this week, toward a goal you choose from 2 to 7 days. Each Monday starts fresh, and missing a day resets nothing.

  • Reminders on your terms

    Choose how often Pip checks in, from once a day to only when you ask. Quiet hours are on by default, Pip stops nudging once you’ve made progress that day, and work tasks only come up during your work hours.

  • Coming back is easy

    Forgot the app for a week? Pip never mentions missed days or overdue tasks. When you come back, Pip is just happy to see you. Even the daily gift doesn’t reset.

  • Rewards for real life

    Your progress sends Pip on adventures that run while the app is closed. Time in the app earns nothing, so there’s no reason to stay. About Pip’s adventures.

What Tiny Pip isn’t

Tiny Pip isn’t a treatment, and it isn’t a substitute for medication, therapy or coaching. It hasn’t been clinically tested, and it doesn’t claim to change ADHD symptoms. What it offers is smaller: a friendly nudge toward one task, a first step that’s easy to take, and no shame when you stop.

If you have questions about ADHD, a doctor or other qualified professional is the right person to talk to. Many people use tools like Tiny Pip alongside the support they already have.

Questions

Is Tiny Pip made for people with ADHD?

Tiny Pip is made for anyone who knows what to do and still can’t start. Many people with ADHD describe that feeling, and the app was designed around it: one task at a time, tiny first steps, short focus sessions and no streaks.

Does Tiny Pip treat ADHD?

No. Tiny Pip is a productivity and self-care app, not ADHD treatment or medical care. It doesn’t diagnose, treat or improve any condition, and it isn’t a replacement for professional support.

Can an app help with task initiation?

An app can’t do the task for you, but it can make starting easier: showing one thing instead of a long list, making the first step small and clear, and rewarding you for starting. That’s what Tiny Pip is built to do.

Will Tiny Pip flood me with notifications?

No. You choose how often Pip checks in, from once a day to only when you ask. Quiet hours are on by default, and once you’ve made progress that day, Pip stops nudging.

What if I forget about the app for a while?

Nothing is lost and nothing resets. There are no streaks, Pip never mentions missed days, and your tasks and notes are waiting where you left them.

Is Tiny Pip free?

Yes. Tiny Pip is free to download and use, and it’s in a free beta on iPhone and Android now. An optional Plus plan is coming at launch, but starting tasks and reminders are always free.
