Breaking tasks down

Don’t finish the whole task. Find the first tiny step.

A big task is hard to start because it isn’t really one thing. Breaking it into steps turns “somehow do all of this” into “do this one small thing next.” Tiny Pip does that with you.

How it works: Tiny Pip breaks tasks into small steps in two ways. For everyday chores, Pip suggests ready-made steps worked out on your phone. For anything else, an optional AI helper (“Help me break this down”) suggests 3 to 7 small steps. Either way, you can edit, reorder or remove any step before saving, or write your own.

From “ugh” to a first step

Adding a task in Tiny Pip takes a few taps:

  1. Name it: “What’s the task?”
  2. Say how big it feels: Quick, A few steps or Time on it.
  3. Say what it’s for: Personal, Work or School.
  4. Get steps: ready-made, from the AI helper, or your own.
  5. Tap I’ve started, or save it for later.

A quick task doesn’t need breaking down; Pip says so: “A quick one! The step is just doing it.” A “time on it” task gets a focus session instead of steps. Everything else gets a short list of small steps.

Two ways to get steps

Ready-made steps cover the chores people put off most. The AI helper covers the rest. Neither is ever applied without your yes.

Ready-made steps for everyday chores

Tiny Pip recognizes nine kinds of everyday task: paying a bill, canceling a subscription, contacting a company, replying to a message, writing something, making a call or a booking, laundry, dishes and tidying. When your task sounds like one of them, Pip says so and suggests a short set of steps, like “Find which account the subscription is on” for canceling a gym membership.

These steps are built into the app, so they work offline and nothing leaves your phone. They’re free, with no limit.

The AI helper, when you ask for it

For a task without ready-made steps, Pip asks, “Want a little help getting started?” Tap Help me break this down and an AI helper suggests 3 to 7 short steps, or a single tiny step for a quick task. If the task is too vague to break down, it may ask you one question first.

The steps appear as a draft. You can edit any of them, move them up or down, remove them or add your own before you save. If the helper can’t make steps right then, for example when you’re offline, Pip says so and you can add your own or try again later. Attempts that fail don’t count toward your limit. What’s sent, and what isn’t

The AI helper only runs when you tap Help me break this down, one task at a time. Right on that screen, Pip says: “Pip sends just these words to an AI helper. Nothing else leaves your phone.”

  • What’s sent: that one task’s words, plus your time zone.
  • What isn’t: your other tasks, habits, check-ins, notes or name.
  • What’s kept: not the task or the steps. Only a random ID and a few counts, so the weekly limit works.

Tiny Pip has no account, and everything else you enter stays on your phone. Details are in the privacy policy.

How many breakdowns you get

Free includes a few AI breakdowns a week, and the app shows how many you have left. Plus, coming at launch, will have no weekly limit. Ready-made steps and your own steps are always unlimited. Asking for steps earns nothing in the game; doing them does.

What makes a first step “tiny”

A good first step is small enough that it’s hard to argue with. It usually has three things in common: it’s physical, it’s specific, and it doesn’t ask you to make a big decision. A few examples of the kind of first step that works:

  • “Do my taxes” → put last year’s tax papers on the table.
  • “Email the landlord” → open a new email and write the subject line.
  • “Clean the bedroom” → put the clothes on the floor into one pile.
  • “Study for the exam” → open the notes for the first chapter.
  • “Plan a birthday dinner for my mom” → open a note and jot down three things she loves to eat. (That one is a real suggestion from the AI helper, shown at the top of this page.)

If a first step still feels like too much, make it smaller. Tiny Pip lets you edit any step at any time.

After the breakdown

Steps aren’t a checklist to dread. They’re one thing at a time, with room to stop.

One step on the screen

Once you start, Tiny Pip shows just the current step, with Done with this step and a Focus timer button. You can skip a step, edit it, add one, or see them all. Starting earns more than finishing, and finishing steps counts too.

When you stop, Pip offers to save a note, and next time Home shows your next step so you can pick it back up.

Questions

Is there an app that breaks tasks into smaller steps?

Yes. Tiny Pip suggests small steps for everyday chores on its own, and for anything else, its optional AI helper suggests 3 to 7 small steps. You can edit, reorder or remove any step before saving.

Does Tiny Pip use AI?

Only if you ask. The AI helper runs when you tap “Help me break this down,” for that one task. Ready-made steps, reminders, check-ins and the game all work on your phone without AI.

Is the AI task breakdown free?

Free includes a few AI breakdowns a week, and failed attempts don’t count. Plus, coming at launch, will have no weekly limit. Ready-made steps and your own steps are always free and unlimited.

What does the AI see?

Just the words of the task you asked about, plus your time zone. Not your other tasks, notes, habits or check-ins. The task and its steps aren’t kept after the steps come back.

Can I write my own steps instead?

Yes. Choose “Add my own steps” at any point, or change the suggested ones. Every step can be edited, skipped or removed later too.

Does it work for work and school tasks?

Yes. When you add a task, you can mark it as personal, work or school, and the steps work the same way.
